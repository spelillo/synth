How to filter a CSV by date range

Orders from last month, transactions between two dates, sign-ups in the past 30 days. Synth loads your CSV into a SQLite database in your browser, so a date filter is one query, and you can download the matching rows as a new CSV. Nothing is installed, and the file never leaves your computer.

The example file

Say you drop an orders.csv like this into Synth. It becomes a table called orders:

order_id | order_date       | total
1        | 2026-01-28       | 40.00
2        | 2026-02-03       | 15.50
3        | 2026-02-17       | 99.90
4        | 2026-02-28 18:45 | 12.00
5        | 2026-03-02       | 22.00
6        | 2026-03-30       | 61.25

Synth keeps every value exactly as it appears in the file, as text. Dates written as YYYY-MM-DD still sort and compare correctly as text, which is why the queries below work. If your dates look like 2/3/2026, skip to dates like 2/3/2026.

Rows between two dates

SELECT order_id, order_date, total
FROM orders
WHERE order_date >= '2026-02-01'
  AND order_date <  '2026-03-01';
order_id | order_date       | total
2        | 2026-02-03       | 15.50
3        | 2026-02-17       | 99.90
4        | 2026-02-28 18:45 | 12.00

Use less than the day after the range rather than BETWEEN. With WHERE order_date BETWEEN '2026-02-01' AND '2026-02-28', order 4 is left out, because 2026-02-28 18:45 comes after 2026-02-28.

Totals by month

strftime('%Y-%m', order_date) turns each date into its month, so you can group by it:

SELECT strftime('%Y-%m', order_date) AS month,
       COUNT(*) AS orders,
       ROUND(SUM(total), 2) AS revenue
FROM orders
GROUP BY month
ORDER BY month;
month   | orders | revenue
2026-01 | 1      | 40.0
2026-02 | 3      | 127.4
2026-03 | 2      | 83.25

The last 30 days

date('now', '-30 days') is the date 30 days before today, so this filter keeps moving as time passes:

SELECT order_id, order_date
FROM orders
WHERE order_date >= date('now', '-30 days');

Swap in '-7 days', '-3 months' or '-1 year' for other windows.

Dates like 2/3/2026

Many exports write dates as month/day/year. As text, 2/3/2026 sorts after 2/28/2026, so a filter like BETWEEN '2/1/2026' AND '2/28/2026' quietly misses February 3rd. Convert each date to YYYY-MM-DD first, then filter on the converted column:

WITH clean AS (
  SELECT *,
         substr(order_date, -4) || '-' ||
         printf('%02d', substr(order_date, 1, instr(order_date, '/') - 1)) || '-' ||
         printf('%02d', substr(order_date, instr(order_date, '/') + 1,
                               length(order_date) - instr(order_date, '/') - 5)) AS iso_date
  FROM orders
)
SELECT order_id, order_date, iso_date, total
FROM clean
WHERE iso_date >= '2026-02-01'
  AND iso_date <  '2026-03-01';
order_id | order_date | iso_date   | total
2        | 2/3/2026   | 2026-02-03 | 15.50
3        | 2/17/2026  | 2026-02-17 | 99.90
4        | 2/28/2026  | 2026-02-28 | 12.00

This handles dates with or without leading zeros, like 2/3/2026 and 02/03/2026. Once iso_date exists, the month and last-30-days queries above work on it too.

Frequently asked questions

How do I filter a CSV by date range?

Drop the CSV into Synth and run SELECT * FROM table WHERE date_column >= '2026-02-01' AND date_column < '2026-03-01'. This works when dates are written as YYYY-MM-DD.

Why does my date filter miss rows?

Synth keeps dates exactly as they appear in the file, as text. Dates like 2/3/2026 don't sort in date order as text, so convert them to YYYY-MM-DD first. The guide has a copy-paste query for that.

Why is the last day of my range missing?

If the column includes a time, 2026-02-28 18:45 comes after 2026-02-28, so BETWEEN leaves it out. Use less than the day after the range instead.

How do I group rows by month?

Use strftime('%Y-%m', date_column) in the SELECT and GROUP BY it. That works on YYYY-MM-DD dates.

Does filtering change my file?

No. A query only creates a result. You can download that result with Download CSV, and the original file is never modified or uploaded.

How big a CSV can I use?
